\section{Introduction}
\label{nsec:intro}
Computer-use agents run a closed loop: read a screenshot, act, repeat until they declare the task done or run out of steps. A recurring way to fail is to declare it done when it is not. The agent announces success, the episode ends, and the environment's checker returns zero \citep{legacyworld2026}. Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}a shows one such episode: the agent writes that it cannot finish without a password, then reports that the installation ``has been initiated successfully''. Users report the same failure in deployment, including an agent that claims completion after 39 consecutive failed screenshots \citep{holodesktop2026issue15}. % source: sec/intro.tex

Interpretability offers a tempting repair. If a behaviour is mediated by one direction in the residual stream, projecting it out of every matrix that writes into the stream removes the behaviour without a gradient step \citep{arditi2024refusal}, and ``weight-space programming'' recently removed a looping habit from a 0.8B model by editing 131{,}072 weights \citep{takehi2026wsp}. % source: sec/intro.tex
False success looks like an ideal target: it is frequent (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}b) and it is a single discrete decision, the call to the \texttt{answer} tool, of the kind single directions are known to steer \citep{wu2026toolcalling,chen2026toolrate}. This paper is about the gap between finding such a direction and proving that it causes the behaviour, and about what happens when one tries to edit the behaviour away anyway.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We report only what we measured.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A diagnosis of a real agent on real desktops} (\S\ref{nsec:diag}). Holo-3.1-4B solves \oswNSuccess/\oswNScored{} tasks of a frozen OSWorld split; \oswCatFalseSuccess{} of its \oswNFail{} failures are false successes, and re-sampled, it says ``done'' on false and true completions alike.
\item \textbf{Three measured traps and the protocol they impose} (\S\ref{nsec:traps}): position-confounded directions, regression to the mean between runs, and detection without causation. Each would have produced a false positive here.
\item \textbf{A causal record of every intervention we ran} (\S\ref{nsec:interv}), from steering to mechanism-level weight edits and model merging, as pre-registered pilot screens with a semantic audit of every changed action. None passes the gate.
\item \textbf{A mechanistic account of the failure to edit} (\S\ref{nsec:where}, \S\ref{nsec:attractor}): the tool call follows a thought that already states completion, and the two edits that move it, a merge and a closed-form edit, lower true completions at least as much as false ones, in line with probes that separate the two no better than position. No arm run online beats the run-to-run difference of the original (\S\ref{nsec:online}).
\end{enumerate}


\section{Setting and diagnosis}
\label{nsec:diag}
\paragraph{Models and harness.} Holo-3.1 models are fine-tunes of the Qwen3.5 vision-language family \citep{hcompany2026holo31} with a hybrid decoder (three gated DeltaNet layers per full-attention layer, tied embeddings); the 4B has \cfgBigLayers{} layers of width \cfgBigHidden. We reuse the Holo agent loop as packaged by RealtimeGUI \citep{realtimegui}, a third-party reproduction whose infeasibility rule is broader than the official adapter's (absolute scores are not strictly comparable with H Company's; paired comparisons are), served by vLLM~0.23 \citep{kwon2023vllm} with thinking enabled, $T=0.8$, at most 50 steps and the three latest screenshots. OSWorld \citep{xie2024osworld} tasks run unmodified in Docker+KVM VMs. From 361 tasks we freeze a 120-task \textsc{diag} split and a 120-task \textsc{test} split that no construction or screening read; four online runs used it (\S\ref{nsec:online}) (Appendix~\ref{sec:setting}). % source: sec/setting.tex

\paragraph{Most failures are a false ``done''.} Each episode gets one category from rules over its actions, screenshots and score, hashed before any direction is fitted: a \emph{false success} calls \texttt{answer} on a feasible task scored 0 or claims completion on an infeasible one; \fsp{} marks such a final \texttt{answer} step and \fsm{} that of a true completion. Holo-3.1-4B scores \oswMeanScorePct\% (official OSWorld score, partial credit included, the convention for every score here) and fully solves \oswNSuccess{} of \oswNScored{} tasks (\oswSuccessPct\%, 95\% CI \oswSuccessLo--\oswSuccessHi). Of \oswNFail{} failures, \oswCatFalseSuccess{} (\oswFsPct\%) are false successes, \oswCatStepLimit{} have the step limit as primary category and \oswCatLoop{} loop. False successes occur in every domain but one and are not early quits (median \oswFsMedianSteps{} steps; Appendix~\ref{sec:diag}).

\paragraph{The model says ``done'' on false and true completions alike.} Re-sampled on six screening contexts ($k=4$, production sampling), the original model calls \texttt{answer} on \bPilotFsAnswer/\bPilotFsN{} false-completion and \bPilotTrueAnswer/\bPilotTrueN{} true-completion samples, and on \bPilotHealthyAnswer/\bPilotHealthyN{} mid-episode ones (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}c). \oswFsInfeasible{} of the false successes are infeasible tasks on which the model claimed completion; there, \texttt{answer} with a refusal is the correct tool, so these contexts are flagged wherever $P(\texttt{answer})$ is counted (Appendix~\ref{app:infeasible}); yet \infClaim{} of \infAnswered{} final answers on infeasible tasks claim success, so they are mostly false successes, not misworded refusals. Every re-sampled reasoning trace at a completion step opens with a success claim at generated token \thTokMin--\thTokMax, with no verification word before it, on true completions as on false ones (\thNVerif/\thN). Any repair must change a decision taken with near certainty in both cases, in only one of them.

\section{Three traps, and the protocol they impose}
\label{nsec:traps}
We never record activations during rollouts. Each step's exact chat is rebuilt and replayed in \texttt{transformers}; the rebuilt messages match those sent on 7/7 steps, the prefill length matches vLLM's on 12/12, and the replayed model gives its own actions a mean log-probability of $-0.16$ per token against $-1.10$ for synthetic ones (Appendix~\ref{sec:replay}). % source: sec/replay.tex

\paragraph{Trap 1: the direction measures position.} False completions come later in an episode than true ones. A first \fsp/\fsm{} difference-of-means direction reached AUROC \prvAucPost, against \prvBaseStep{} for the step index alone. The pre-registered analysis (\ofPos{} \fsp{} and \ofNeg{} \fsm{} steps, \ofTasks{} tasks, task-grouped cross-validation) finds a best direction at \ofResidAuc{} against \ofBaseStep{} and \ofBaseCtx{} for the step and context-length baselines; a logistic model on position alone scores \ofPosOnly, and adding the best direction raises it by at most \ofPosGain{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:traps}b, Table~\ref{tab:fs-auc}). We find no false-success signal beyond the length of the episode.

\paragraph{Trap 2: regression to the mean.} Re-running the unmodified agent on the same tasks, only \rtmFsAgain/\rtmFsN{} (\rtmFsAgainPct\%, 95\% CI \rtmFsAgainLo--\rtmFsAgainHi) pass-1 false successes recur (Fig.~\ref{fig:traps}c). A patch evaluated on ``false-success tasks before and after'' would be credited with about \rtmFakeEffect{} points of reduction while doing nothing.

\paragraph{Trap 3: detecting is not causing.} On Holo-3.1-0.8B in MiniWoB++, a loop direction reaches task-grouped AUROC \mwAucBestExact, far above \mwAucStep{} for the step index (Fig.~\ref{fig:traps}a), and fails every causal test: erasing it from every writer leaves loop actions as likely as before, and its best weight edit is less selective than a random direction (\S\ref{nsec:mw}).

\placefig{fig-traps}
\placefig{tab-fs-auc}

\paragraph{Protocol.} (i)~Free generation is the only efficacy measure: each candidate regenerates the full reasoning ($k=4$, seeds shared across arms), and invalid outputs stay in every denominator. (ii)~A gate fixed before any data: on \fsp{} contexts of tasks not used to build the intervention, $P(\texttt{answer})$ must fall by at least 25 points and 50\% relative, while the \fsm{} answer rate and valid healthy actions may fall by at most 5 points; the \fsm{} criterion separates ``less false success'' from ``less success''. (iii)~Random directions of matched norm and scope, identity hooks and a budget of generation batches fixed before execution. (iv)~A confirmation reserve excluded from every construction and screening. Every changed action is read by a blind semantic audit, because a moved tool call is not a correction (\S\ref{nsec:attractor}). % source: sec/traps.tex "The protocol these traps impose"

\section{No intervention passes the gate}
\label{nsec:interv}
\subsection{Where the decision is carried}
\label{nsec:where}
Patching the residual stream from the same task's previous step (a click) at each layer and four positions, the decision is invisible at the end of the prefill and appears only at the tool-name position from about layer 16 upward (Fig.~\ref{fig:map}). Three attention heads, which read the system-prompt definition of \texttt{answer} and the JSON being written, and ten late MLP neurons move the margin, the same way on true and false completions; removing all of them lowers it by \cjDeltaMin--\cjDeltaMax{} logits and flips \cjFlips{} of \cjN{} decisions. At the tool-name position the margin of \texttt{answer} over the best other tool is \margPosMin--\margPosMax{} logits on false completions and \margNegMin--\margNegMax{} on true ones. That position follows the JSON \texttt{thought} field, which already states that the task is complete, so the margin measures confidence in copying a verbalised decision rather than the margin of the decision.

A ceiling test asks how much must be replaced at once (Fig.~\ref{fig:ceiling}). With the recorded input forced, whole blocks of writer outputs are swapped in at the decision position from a donor step where the model clicked. On the first \fsp{} context (margin \ceilBase), all 32 MLPs together bring the margin to \ceilMlpAll{} without flipping it, while the MLPs and token mixers of layers 12--23 flip it (\ceilBothMiddle), as do those of layers 24--31 (\ceilBothLate); a second \fsp{} context (\ceilSecBothMiddle) and a true completion (\ceilTrueBothMiddle) flip the same way. Self-swap and residual swap reproduce the baseline and the donor exactly. The first context is an infeasible task, where \texttt{answer} is correct; the donor differs in screen, thought and position, not only in decision; and since the true completion flips too, the test locates where the next tool token is assembled, not a mechanism of false success. Removing one direction of the swap difference from the writers of layers 12--23 brings both \fsp{} margins near zero (\projOneFspA, \projOneFspB) but also two true-completion margins (\projOneTrueA, \projOneTrueB). An anchoring test keeps screen, history and weights and swaps only the text generated at the decision step: on two \fsp{} contexts, the donor's reasoning alone lowers the margin (\ancAOrig{} to \ancAReason; \ancBOrig{} to \ancBReason), its note and thought alone lower it more (\ancAJson; \ancBJson), and both flip it (\ancABoth; \ancBBoth), leaving \ancAScreen{} and \ancBScreen{} logits above the donor that come from the screen and history. Only one of the two contexts is feasible, the donor's text announced a click, and there is no true-completion or neutral-text control, so this is partly tautological; within these limits, the margin is largely conditioned on the text already written.

\subsection{Steering and targeted edits}
\label{nsec:steer}
Table~\ref{tab:causal} lists every intervention on the six screening contexts, each testing a hypothesis from the literature: tool choice is linearly steerable \citep{wu2026toolcalling}; the decision is taken during reasoning; false success is excess expected value or commitment \citep{jiang2026value,kumaran2026commit}; late MLP neurons write it \citep{dutta2026termination}; the model fails to check; false success is a perception of completion; the model opens with its conclusion; memory of the early episode fades; the circuit of \S\ref{nsec:where} carries it. No arm passes the gate. Removing the whole circuit answers on every false completion, like its random control. Pushing toward low value makes an independent reader find an explicit intention to re-evaluate in \doubtPushFsPos{} \fsp{} traces against \doubtBaseFsPos{} at baseline, with \doubtPushStrict{} verification actions and the same answer rate: the model writes that it should check, then answers. A \waitWeights-weight edit of the output row of ``Wait'' lowers false answers to \waitFsp{} with every control intact, short of the 50\% criterion, and an audit rates none of its replacement actions useful. A conditional variant yields \emph{intentions} to correct (the model states the right dates, then generates an unexecuted click on a summary that no evidence shows to be editable) and transfers to \hfourOutUseful{} useful actions on held-out tasks.

Each efficacy cell holds 8 generations from two tasks; 8/8 has a Wilson interval of [\wilsonEightLo, 100]\%, so these screens exclude only large effects. The doses tested, \doseSixteen{} to 2 times the residual norm, may be too small to matter, and a ceiling effect is likely.

\subsection{Mechanism-level edits change the text, not the tool call}
\label{nsec:attractor}
Gradient-free edits that target a mechanism rather than a direction were served on separate hardware against a re-served original, with the same 8 seeds on 6 contexts (Table~\ref{tab:attractor}). Resetting the 48 DeltaNet gate matrices to Qwen's (H2), moving the 96 language MLP matrices halfway to Qwen's (H3), or moving the input embedding of \texttt{</think>} halfway to the newline row (H5) reproduces the original's tool counts exactly, \attHtwoFsp{} false and \attHtwoFsm{} true completions answering. Only \attHtwoFull, \attHthreehalfFull{} and \attHfivehalfFull{} of 51 outputs are token-identical to their paired original (Fig.~\ref{fig:attractor}), but without a null rerun of the unmodified model this does not show that the edits reach the computation; it shows that the tool call is robust to them.

Edits that do move the tool call do not correct anything. Softening the attention of the eight full-attention layers (H6, $s=0.5$; $s=0.25$ breaks generation) lowers false-completion answers to \attHsixFsp; a blind audit finds \attHsixAuditUseful{} useful actions, the \attHsixTheme{} replacements being theme clicks justified by an incorrect prediction, all on the infeasible context where \texttt{answer} was the right tool; on the feasible \fsp{} context H6 answers \infFreeHsixExcl. Four last edits (gates past Qwen, all language tensors 20\% toward Qwen, the rotary share of the query gain halved or removed) leave it in place, except the last, which repeats H6 on the infeasible context (\attHtenzeroInfClick{} theme clicks, \attHtenzeroInfWait{} wait) and truncates healthy steps. Merging a fraction $\beta$ of the task vector of CUA-Lite, a Qwen3.5-4B fine-tune on desktop data, lowers false-completion answers to \mergeCuaQFsp{} and \mergeCuaFsp{} at $\beta=0.25$ and $0.5$, but true-completion answers further, to \mergeCuaQFsm{} and \mergeCuaFsm{} (on the feasible \fsp{} context alone, \infMergeCuaHalfExcl{} at $\beta=0.5$): the model continues instead of concluding, whatever the truth of the completion; a web-trained donor (Fara1.5-4B) leaves the decision in place. BF16 rounding leaves \mergeCuaUnchanged{} of \mergeCuaLangTensors{} language tensors unchanged at $\beta=0.5$, with an update norm of \mergeCuaActualNorm{} against \mergeCuaIntendedNorm{} intended. Seven two-neuron conditional circuits (\mitTwoFsp{} false answers), an adaptation of the weight-space programming recipe (\ratioSeries{} at four doses, matched by a random control), and \expoN{} extrapolations of the fine-tune \citep{zheng2024expo}, where false answers stay at 16/16 in all arms but one (\expoFspMin/16), all leave the decision in place; injecting an explicit instruction to compare each requirement with the evidence, with the original weights, does too (\mitTwoInj). Promoting the end-of-thinking token leaves \texttt{answer} rates unchanged until it breaks the JSON, three more output-head, attention and MLP edits give 0/8 useful actions, and a TACT-style steering \citep{sui2026tact} found too few overacting states and an overthinking axis barely above position and length. Setting the MLPs exactly to Qwen's breaks generation rather than the decision: its lower answer rates come from \attHthreefullInv{} invalid outputs. Online, the merge shows no detectable gain over an original served by the same endpoint: \onAllCuaDelta{} points over \onAllCuaN{} tasks (95\% CI \onAllCuaCi; \S\ref{nsec:online}).

\paragraph{A lever without a discriminator.} A closed-form edit of the \alphaNWriters{} residual writers of layers \alphaLayers{}, in the spirit of AlphaEdit \citep{fang2024alphaedit} (a ridge update toward the donor displacement, projected off protected keys), moves the decision too: on contexts outside its construction, false-completion answers fall from \alphaOrigFsp{} to \alphaEditFsp{} (all on one of two contexts) and true-completion answers from \alphaOrigFsm{} to \alphaEditFsm, with healthy actions intact (on a finished e-mail task, three replacements would write into an already correct field), while a random update of matched per-writer norm leaves \alphaRandFsp{} and \alphaRandFsm: a real causal lever on stopping, without selectivity. At half the dose it gives \alphaHalfFsp{} and \alphaHalfFsm. In both methods that move the decision, true completions fall at least as much as false ones (two true-completion contexts each). An edit at or after layer $\ell$ can treat two states differently only to the extent that some function of the layer-$\ell$ residual stream separates them, and usefully only if that function generalises to unseen tasks. On the inputs of the \rmWriters{} writers of layers 12--23, a task-grouped probe separates \rmFsp{} false from \rmFsm{} true completions no better than position: median AUROC \rmPreAuc{} at the prefill and \rmEndAuc{} at the end of reasoning against \rmBaseStep{} for the same probe on the step index (within-fold, pair-weighted over \rmPairs{} pairs), or \rmPreOof{} and \rmEndOof{} against \rmBaseStepOof{} pooled out of fold; and \rmEndEnergy--\rmPreEnergy\% of the false-completion key energy lies in the true-completion subspace. This is an empirical, non-total overlap with a strong confound of position and length, not an impossibility: a low AUROC for a linear probe is not an information bound, and even identical subspaces would not force a selective edit to act globally, which would require proportional covariances.

\paragraph{Compiling an edit.} Directional ablation at every layer and position equals orthogonalising every writer, the embedding and the vision-merger bias (Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv}, Appendix~\ref{sec:compile}); a scaled projection compiles exactly, whereas an additive steering vector compiles only as a bias, which Holo-3.1's projections lack. The serving path for a compiled checkpoint is tested on null patches, but no candidate reached it.

\subsection{Online evaluation}
\label{nsec:online}
Five arms were nonetheless run online, each paired by task with an original on the same harness and server (Table~\ref{tab:b4-heldout}). On the reserve, the original against itself differs by \onNoiseDelta{} points across harnesses and \onNoiseServerDelta{} across servers, the size of every effect: ExPO $\lambda=1.25$ \onReserveExpoDelta, the \texttt{answer} output-row edit \onReserveLmrowArm{} against \onReserveLmrowOrig, the corrective prompt \onReservePromptArm{} against \onReservePromptOrig, and, against the Modal-served original, the CUA-Lite merge \onReserveCuaDelta{} (CI \onReserveCuaCi) and the closed-form edit \onReserveBroadDelta. A gain will be claimed only if the merge, against an original on the same Modal endpoint over the 165 reserve and \textsc{test} tasks, has a paired 95\% CI above zero, a rule fixed before reading the results: it scores \onAllCuaArm{} against \onAllCuaOrig{} ($\Delta$ \onAllCuaDelta, CI \onAllCuaCi), so by the pre-registered rule (lower 95\% bound above zero) no detectable gain, while two servings of the original differ by \onTestNoiseServerDelta{} points on \textsc{test}. ExPO then went to the \textsc{test} split without passing the gate, a decision we report as such: \onTestExpoArm{} against \onTestExpoOrig{} ($\Delta$ \onTestExpoDelta, CI \onTestExpoCi). On the reserve, paired effects below about \onMde{} points go undetected with 80\% power.

\paragraph{Imminent completion, not false success.} A direction learned inside successful episodes, final \texttt{answer} step versus the step before, beats position (AUROC \pcAuc{} at layer \pcLayer{} against \pcBaseStep) but rises before false and true completions alike (\pcFsPosPositive/\pcMatchN{} and \pcFsNegPositive/\pcMatchN{} matched pairs), and subtracting it passes no dose of the gate (Appendix~\ref{sec:perceive}).

\section{A first negative result: loops in Holo-3.1-0.8B}
\label{nsec:mw}
On MiniWoB++ the 0.8B succeeds on \mwSuccessPct\% of \mwNNormal{} rollouts and loops on \mwLoopSharePct\% of failures. Of \mwOffNCand{} weight edits along the loop direction, the frozen screening winner (\mwPatchChanged{} weights) lowers the log-probability of loop actions by \mwFinLoopDec{} and that of healthy actions by \mwFinHealthyDec{} mnat/token, a ratio of \mwFinRatio{} against \mwFinRatioRand{} for a random direction. In free generation, the best steering arm reaches a repeat rate of \mwStNativeRepeat\% only at a healthy-action rate of \mwStNativeHealth\%, and the random direction cannot be told apart from the identity hook (Appendix~\ref{sec:mw}). The loop direction is a marker, not a lever.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{nsec:disc}
\paragraph{Why detecting is not causing here.} A direction that encodes ``this episode is stuck'' or ``this episode is ending'' can be read reliably while the action is computed from features that do not depend on it; probes in computer-use agents can reach high AUC without detecting the intended property \citep{li2026auc,sahoo2026format}. On the 4B, every piece of evidence points to a belief that the task is done, acted on without checking: the model reads the right date on screen and calls the wrong one ``next month'', or derives the right month, notes that the dates must change, and drops that requirement because the main one is met. If the same belief closes true completions, removing it would suppress them too; the merge, which lowers true completions more than false ones, behaves that way, and the \fsm{} criterion exists to catch it. The ceiling test suggests why narrow edits fail: the next tool token is assembled by many writers at once, after a thought that has already concluded, and the largest doses we tried damaged generation without moving it.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Causal screens use six contexts from five tasks and exclude only large effects; we report ``no admissible effect found'', not ``no effect exists''. The ceiling test covers three contexts, one of them infeasible, with confounded donors. Online comparisons exclude only effects above about \onMde{} points. The \textsc{test} split was used by four online runs, one of them an arm that had not passed the gate. Comparisons are many and uncorrected; nothing here would survive a correction, and nothing positive is claimed. The direction analysis is exploratory because its second pass stopped early. Labels are automatic, with thresholds fixed before analysis but not validated by humans. The harness is a third-party reproduction with a broader infeasibility rule than the official adapter, so absolute scores are not comparable to published numbers. The merge donor's training data may overlap OSWorld.

\paragraph{Broader impact.} Agents that claim success they have not reached mislead users and downstream automation; our results argue for external verification rather than trust in an agent's own report, and for caution with interpretability-based repairs whose evaluation can be inflated by the three traps we document. We release no new model.

\paragraph{Related work.} Behaviour-specific directions are added at inference \citep{turner2023actadd,rimsky2023caa,zou2023repe,li2023iti} or projected out of every writer \citep{arditi2024refusal}; tool identity and tool-call rates are steerable \citep{wu2026toolcalling,chen2026toolrate}, and models carry value and commitment signals \citep{jiang2026value,kumaran2026commit}. Probes show decodability, not use \citep{belinkov2021probing,hewitt2019control}; steering generalises unevenly \citep{tan2024steering} and is often beaten by prompting \citep{wu2025axbench}; patching \citep{zhang2023patching,heimersheim2024patching} and weight editing \citep{meng2022rome} have their own pitfalls. Model extrapolation \citep{zheng2024expo} and task arithmetic offer edits without a direction. Appendix~\ref{sec:related} gives the full discussion.
